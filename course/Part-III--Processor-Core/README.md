## Part III: The Automated Computer – Memory and Control

Incredible work completing Part II. Take a moment to step back and appreciate what you have built. You have forged the complete brain of our compute: a powerful and versatile Arithmetic Logic Unit that can perform math, execute logic, and even report on the status of its own calculations. You have built a genuine, manually-operated processor.

But a computer is more than a processor. It doesn't wait for a human to flip levers for every single step. A true computer can follow a list of instructions, a program, all on its own.

In this final core Part of the course, we give our machine its soul. The theme is **Automation**. We are going to build the final architectural components that separate a static calculator from a dynamic, living computer. We will give it a memory to hold its thoughts and a heartbeat to drive it forward.

### Our Mission for Part III

This part will see us construct the final pieces of the puzzle and assemble them into a single, cohesive, self-running system.

-   **In Module 10 (The Processor's Scratchpad),** we will first tackle the concept of "state." We'll build a circuit called a register that can remember a value, giving our ALU a "scratchpad" to store its results.

-   **In Module 11 (Addressable Storage),** we will scale that single scratchpad into a full "notebook." We'll construct a 16x4-bit Random Access Memory (RAM) module, allowing our computer to store and retrieve multiple values from unique addresses.

-   **In Module 12 (The Control Unit & Programmable Logic),** we will build the machine's conductor. We'll create a clock to provide a steady pulse, define a language for our computer to understand, and build a Control Unit that can automatically fetch, decode, and execute a sequence of instructions, including logical jumps.

By the end of this Part, you will have achieved the ultimate goal of this course. You will have orchestrated a collection of simple components into a machine that can read and execute a program from memory, all without your intervention.

Let's begin by giving our computer a memory.
